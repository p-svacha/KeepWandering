- Add more generic/simple day encounters. Many current day encounters are quite complex in that they give many different options and paths. Normal, random day encounters are allowed to be simple, with simple options and outcomes. Try not to give too many options at once. And if you do, make sure not more than 3 or available without any requirements.
- Increase item effects. Currently there's many encounter options with item slots that don't have a big effect. Items should have a big effect generally (see item section for new system).
- More fixed outcome options. Skill checks are cool, but don't overuse them. FixedOutcome options are often very fine too. Try to usually have both skill checks and fixed outcomes available.
- Generally as a rule of thumb I think it would be good for an encounter step to have:
  - 1 FixedOutcome option without requirements (safe, consistent option, usually with no, neutral, or slightly positive/negative effect)
  - 1 SkillCheck option with an item slot for a tag for dynamic difficulty reduction (gambling option, but can be made safe if good item available, with strong positives on success and strong negatives on fail). If more than 1 available, they have to be very clearly distinct in what the success effects are!
  - 1 FixedOutcome option with requirements (generally just safe "good" options, but locked behind requirements). If more than 1 available, both requirements and effect have to be clearly distict.
  - Of course this a RULE OF THUMB. Other combinations are allowed depending on encounter and step, but this is a good basic guideline to follow for fun gameplay, strategic decisions.
- Add more REQUIREMENTS. Options with requirements that make them unavailable if not met are cool, since they give a clear goal/mini-quest. Especially in FixedOutcome options they can be used to give a clear "best option" that requires specific criteria.
  - Also add skills/stats as possible requirements (i.e. Dexterity 5)
  - Maybe even remove requirements completely for Skill Check Options. If an option is gated behind a requirement, a success should maybe be guaranteed? Especially since they still have a fixed "cost" now with the durability system.
  - With the new item tag level system, also add possibility to require a specific level (i.e. "Requires Lvl 3 Lockpick").
  - Possibility of a requiring a SPECIFIC item should still stay.

General Design Philosophy:
- All choices presented to the player should have clear different paths for different strategies and offer interesting choices and ways for progression. Design in favour of fun gameplay is more important than any realism. Many encounters should give inherent "mini-quests", basically simple requirements, chains of events that give the player always a clear next goal (don't even have to be REAL quests in the quest log, just inherent things the player will need/want to do). Also day encounters that directly have interconnections with location and time, rather than being fully isolated. The persistent encounter system should be used to its fullest extent here with routing, backtracking, etc. Some examples for this:
  - Persisten traders as a day encounter that buy / sell specific items, so the player can also go back for that item or gold.
  - A spot where something is buried. Only possible interaction is with a shovel, so player will want to come back with a shovel.
  - A lit flare encounter that doesn't do anything, but the player learns that a care package will land here in 10 days.
  - Landmarks that are visible from the start that give a very clear indicator of what could be found there (i.e. fuel station for fuel, supermarket for food, pharmacy for medical items, etc.)
  - Simple choices like:
    - Choose 1 out of 3 items to receive (or lose).
  - Clear signals to the player of how a specific item can be acquired (maybe dedicated item pages in the handbook?)
  - Clear signals to the player of how a specific stat can be increased (maybe dedicated stat pages in the handbook?)

Items:
- Rework the current tag system:
  - Currently items have a tag and an optional "value modifier" on that tag to determine their effectiveness on that tag (i.e. bone has tag weapon, but -20 because it is a bad weapon).
  - Currently encounter options determine how much difficulty an item slot reduces if filled (modified by that tag modifier)
  - This works but can be reworked to a more elegant system:
  - Instead of having a flat value modifier and encounter options determining item effectiveness/difficulty reduction, make a general level/tier system:
    - Each tag on an item has a level/tier (1-5). (i.e. a knife is a lvl3 weapon, a lvl1 lockpick and lvl4 cutting tool).
	- Encounter options no longer have to determine difficulty reduction per item slot
	- Instead the level determines that. 1 = 20% difficulty reduction, 2 = 40%, 3 = 60%, 4 = 80%, 5 = 100%
	- Should also make it way easier to understand, easier to display in the UI, and less work to design encounter options (as you only have to set the tag for an option, and the difficulty calculation is standardized from there).
- Add durability system (see above)
  - Initial durability is randomized between 1-4.
- Make consumable effects more clear
  - Consumable system is fully detached from tag level system.
  - Just make it really clear in item description what happens / can happen if consumed (no cryptic information).
  - Like exact calories, chance of food poisoning, etc.
- Clean up loot tables so they are smaller and more varied between encounters / biomes
- Individual items should all have their own identity and use cases.
- Allow drag on drop from items that can tend wounds to tendable wounds.
- Allow cooking food (maybe add this option to new generic evening encounter)
